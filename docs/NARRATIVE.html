The one thing to know before reading. There are TWO supernatural powers in this story and they are easy to confuse, because both of them raise the dead.

  • The thing in the archaeologist came through the gate. It raises everything buried and it will not let anything stay down. It made the player a corpse that walks.
  • The gatekeeper is one of the Ancients, imprisoned in Area 51. He does not raise the dead. He stands ONE dead man up, over and over, because he has picked him. He is the reason the player keeps getting back onto his feet after each death.

Meeting 1 only had the first. Meeting 2 added the second. Both are true at once, and the whole ending depends on the difference.

PART ZERO - BEFORE THE GAME#

None of this is playable. About a third of the story happens before the title screen and the player learns it in quarters, out of order, from four jars. Writing it down first is the only way the four fragments can be authored to add up.

The Ancients, and what they were guarding#

Before Egypt there were people here, and they built the gate.

They did not build it to open. They built it because it was already there, and what they built around it was a lock and a post and a rule. There is another universe on the far side and the two have been at war for as long as either has been able to reach the other. The Ancients' job was that nothing crossed.

(Quoted as said. "The Ancients" is the name we settled on afterwards, and the transcripts are left alone so the record still shows the words in the room.)

PROPOSAL

PROPOSAL, mine, and it reconciles those two lines rather than choosing between them. The Ancients are what got remembered as the Egyptian gods. The Egyptians did not invent the pantheon. They came later, found the works of people who had been here first, and copied them, and the names we use now - Anubis, Ammit, Apep, Sekhmet, Set - are Egyptian names for something older that was already standing at its post. So both meeting-2 statements hold: the Egyptian gods were real gatekeepers, AND the good spirit is one of the ancient people. Same class of being, two names, and the older name is the true one.

This was already proposed in WORLDS.md as World 2's design principle before meeting 2 happened. Meeting 2 promoted it from decoration to load-bearing.

The Ancients made tools for the job. The tools are cut with the same hieroglyphs as the pyramid, because the pyramid is theirs, and the Egyptians copied the writing too. One of those tools is the Raygun, and it is not a weapon. It is the key that locks the gate.

One of the Ancients was left on watch. He is the gatekeeper. He has been at his post since before there was an Egypt, he does not age, and standing next to that door for that long has done something to him: he has been morphing into something that is no longer entirely one of the Ancients either.

The Egyptians, and what a pyramid is for#

The Egyptians built on top. They built the tombs, they buried their dead under the sand in every direction, and they put a queen's chapel deep in the structure with a serdab in it, which is the sealed room where the part of a person that is not the body is kept.

They did not know what was underneath them, or they knew it as a religion. Both readings are available and the game never settles it.

PROPOSAL

PROPOSAL, mine. The queen is HETEPHERES, a real Fourth Dynasty queen buried at Giza whose tomb's famous feature is its serdab, with a row of women carved into the wall. She is dead and she is not a character. Her name is on a cartouche in the serdab and somebody struck it out. See "What happened to Hetepheres" at the end of this document, because meeting 2 changed what her name is FOR and the change is worth reading on its own.

Why it is no longer guarded#

The government got there first, years earlier. When they first entered the pyramid they found a man standing in front of a door, and the man was not a man. They took him. He is in Area 51 now, in a chamber built to hold him, and Area 51 itself was built around a second gate they had already found on their own soil.

Removing the gatekeeper is what started the anomaly. The pull the instruments picked up is the door leaning open.

What the government knew, and what it sent#

The government knew exactly what was down there. It had the gatekeeper in a cell and it had a second gate of its own to study. What it did not know was what the gate would do to a person.

So it sent people to find out, under cover of discovery. A team of scientists and archaeologists, briefed to investigate an anomaly, actually dispatched as test subjects. The cover was real enough that the team believed it. The instruction was to reach the anomaly and open what was in front of it.

WORLD 1 - THE TOMB#

The player is outside the pyramid, on the spot where he died, and the game begins.

WORLD BOUNDARY. World 1 runs from the first frame in the courtyard to the moment the player climbs into the eleventh niche in the Serdab. It is complete, shippable and comprehensible on its own. A player who finishes World 1 and never plays another minute has had a whole story: a tomb, a job, a friend, a name, and a room at the bottom with the wrong person sitting in it.

What World 1 is for#

Three things, and everything in it serves one of them.

  1. Establish that this place has rules, that the rules do not hold him, and that the one person who could tell him why stops being able to.
  2. Give him a quarter of his memory back four times, so that the player learns the origin in Part Zero in fragments, in the order the map hands them over rather than the order they happened.
  3. Spend nothing. Everything about the gate, the government, the other side and the gatekeeper's imprisonment is planted here and confirmed later. World 1 is the last time the world looks knowable and that belief is the asset the other two worlds spend.

There is a doorway into the pyramid and it is blocked. He came out of it four days ago at a dead run, bleeding, and something came out behind him. He does not remember that. The first thing the player buys in this game is the way back into the building he died escaping.

PROPOSAL, mine, and it is the single biggest cost saving available in either meeting. Meeting 2 asks for a CUT SCENE at the gate, in World 2, delivering all of this at once. A cut scene is technology this game does not have and the design has refused twice on principle.

Move the flashbacks onto the jars and there is no cut scene. What a fragment can actually be, priced honestly: the game has a curtain that holds at full black for two drawn frames, a post chain with a grade and a vignette, an unlit material path, and no character models. And at 02:42 the archaeologist IS the evil spirit and the evil spirit is "a real Egyptian god", while the good one is one of the ancient people. Hetepheres is a real queen, not a god, and there is no longer anyone to rescue.

She was chosen for good reasons that have not gone away: her tomb's defining feature is a serdab, the map has an unused Serdab, her name means roughly "she loves life" which is the diagnosis of a thing that will not let anything stay dead, and she was buried in a sarcophagus originally cut for her mother, which is a person in a vessel that was not hers.

RECOMMENDATION: she stays, demoted from spirit to NAME.

Hetepheres is the dead queen whose tomb this is. She is not a character, not an antagonist, and nobody rescues her. Her name is what the struck cartouche in the serdab says, restoring an effaced royal name is a real thing archaeologists actually do, and it is what the archaeologist was down here doing when everything went wrong. It is a completely mundane, completely correct motivation that needs no supernatural justification at all.

What that buys:

  • The player's errand keeps its cover story and gains its real one. He thinks he is finishing his friend's academic work. He is actually charging a machine with four of his colleagues. Both are true, the game never winks, and WORLD-1.md's best structural sentence - "the player is doing her work and calling it a rescue" - survives meeting 2 without a word changed.
  • The antagonist becomes genuinely nameless in World 1, which is what WORLD-1.md wanted anyway. It is a sign, not a noun. The mark on the walls belongs to the thing that struck the name out.
  • The vessel motif gets its third term for free. A queen in her mother's sarcophagus, an archaeologist with something else inside her, and a man walking around in a body that a spirit stood up.
  • Nothing already written has to be thrown away. The four glyphs, the Serdab cartouche, the end card THE NAME IS NOT HERE, all intact and all meaning something slightly worse than they did.

The alternative, and it is real: NEITH. If the owner wants the antagonist to literally be a named Egyptian goddess, as meeting 2 says, Neith is the one. She is genuinely pre-dynastic and therefore older than most of the pantheon, which fits the old-people layer. She is a guardian of the dead. And she is already wired into this map: she is one of the four goddesses standing behind the four canopic sons, guardian of the third jar. Which would mean the player returns his four dead colleagues into her custody, one at a time, personally.

The reason not to reach for it in World 1 is that naming her early costs the nameless-sign device, which is one of the strongest things in the world. My recommendation is to hold Neith in reserve and let World 2 name her, at the moment the archaeologist reveals what she is. The name arrives in the same breath as the reveal, in the world where the gods are carvings older than the gods, and World 1 never has to say a word.

QUESTION FOR THE OWNER: is Hetepheres a name on a wall (recommended), or is she the antagonist? If she is the antagonist, the "real Egyptian god" line has to go, because she is a queen. If she is a name on a wall, the antagonist stays nameless until World 2 and my recommendation for its name is NEITH.

WHAT WORLD 1 NEEDS FROM THE MAP LANE#

The owner's instruction: more space, and the descent should shape the map.

The full brief - every dramatic purpose World 1 needs, which existing room carries it, what goes deeper and in what order, where the fixed beats land, and four warnings - is in docs/WORLD-1.md under "THE MAP BRIEF". It is written in purposes rather than geometry and it specifies no coordinates, because the map lane knows constraints this lane does not.

Three findings from it belong in the story document, because they are conclusions about the STORY rather than about the map.

1. The story does not need more interior rooms. Every dramatic purpose in World 1 already has a home among the nine that exist. Adding rooms to make the map feel bigger would produce exactly the thing the owner is complaining about: a space that exists because the map wanted another room.

2. What the story does need is outdoors. Meeting 2 put a government, a team of seven, equipment and a classified file into World 1's back story, and there is currently nowhere outside for any of it to be. The expedition camp is the one genuinely new space World 1 requires, and it is the only place in the entire trilogy where the institution that caused all of this is physically present before Area 51.

3. World 1's floor is the Serdab, and what is under it is stone Egyptians did not cut, and under that is the gate his team opened. That is the answer to "where does World 1 end and what is beneath it", and it is the only geographic fact the map lane needs from the story.

RECOMMENDATION

RECOMMENDATION on the vertical question, mine: ONE real descent in World 1, not nine. Author the per-room depth number as data and build exactly one place where the player physically goes down and can feel it, at the Act 2 to Act 3 break. If every room is a floor lower the player stops registering any of them, and the world already spends its one vertical gasp on a shaft that points UP and delivers nothing. One built descent against one built ascent is a shape. Nine floors is a number.
