# THE PAPER CUT - World 1 as the player actually experiences it

The owner's ask, 2026-08-08: *"maybe have, like, a paper cut of the whole story
in gameplay so that we make sure it's coherent. Write out the gameplay story and
make sure it makes sense, and once it's polished and we're happy, make sure the
map and the game follow it."*

**This is not the story. `docs/NARRATIVE.md` is the story**, told A to Z in the
order it happened, and about a third of it takes place before the title screen.
This is the other thing: every beat a PLAYER hits, in the order they hit it, from
pressing BEGIN to the last card. Nothing is included here that a player cannot
see or hear.

That difference is the entire point. A story document can be coherent and the
game still incoherent, because the player meets the beats in a different order,
separated by twenty minutes of fighting, with no narrator.

> **STATUS is verified against the code, not against a plan.** Every row was
> checked in `src/` on 2026-08-08. Where something is written but unbuilt it says
> so, because a paper cut that lists intentions as if they were beats is exactly
> the document that makes an incoherent game look finished.
>
> | | |
> |---|---|
> | **LIVE** | a player experiences it today |
> | **WIRED** | in the build, boots clean, gated by a suite - but no one has watched it happen end to end at real frame rates |
> | **BUILT, UNSEEN** | the geometry exists and is measured, and the player cannot get eyes on it. Worse than BUILDING, because it looks finished from every angle except the only one that counts |
> | **BUILDING** | a lane is on it right now |
> | **WRITTEN** | authored in a doc, no code |
> | **GAP** | nobody has written it and the sequence needs it |

# COHERENCE FINDINGS

Reading it in player order rather than story order surfaces six problems. Four
are being built. **Two are genuine holes nobody has written**, and the first is
serious.

## 1. CLOSED 2026-08-08 - THE SIEGE

**The owner's fix, verbatim:** *"What if she's trapped inside of that room and
she can't come out until all the dead are gone. And the dead are gone after wave
twenty-five. So I go through this big fight to get her out."*

**She is sealed in the Serdab and the twenty-five waves are the siege.** That is
World 1's want, and it is now stated on the objective panel above the step.

### Why this is the right answer and not just a good one

**The game already enforced it.** `ui/ending.js` has gated World 1 on three
conditions since the day it shipped, and not one of them had a reason attached:

| The gate | The reason it now has |
|---|---|
| The run has concluded **and the field is clear** | The dead are gone, so she can come out |
| Four sons are home | The machine is charged, so the chapel is unsealed |
| The player is inside the Serdab | You are there to meet her |

A premise that arrives and makes three existing conditions coherent at once was
not invented to fit; it was the thing the design was already shaped around.

It also converts the wave counter from inherited genre structure into plot.
Twenty-five waves used to be how long the game is. **Now it is how long she has
to wait.**

### What it changed, and the one thing it improved by accident

**The trail is no longer a person walking ahead.** It cannot be - she has been
sealed since before the title screen. So the chalk numbers, the survey pegs, the
rope in the shaft and the notebook with the niches numbered wrong are **four days
of her own archaeology on the way down**, which is why the work gets fresher the
deeper he goes: he is following it toward its end, and she is at the end of it,
on the wrong side of a door.

That is a straight improvement. It is what the dressing literally is, and it
stops requiring a living person to be permanently just out of sight.

### How her voice reaches him - decided, and never explained on screen

**A serdab is architecturally a sealed room with slots cut through the wall so
the thing inside can look out.** That is the real definition and `NARRATIVE.md`
already uses it. Sound goes both ways through slots and the tomb carries it.

**The game says nothing about this, ever** - the same way it never mentions that
the interior is bigger than the building. The alternative considered and rejected
was that she is only sealed from the Kindling onward, which has cleaner physics
and gives the first fifteen waves no reason to exist again.

### Built

- **`ui/objective.js`** gained a standing goal above the ladder: SHE CANNOT OPEN
  THE DOOR UNTIL THE DEAD ARE DOWN, with the remaining wave count beside it. It
  is a READOUT of the director's own counter, not a script, so it cannot promise
  something the game does not deliver. `FINAL_WAVE` is now exported from
  `enemies/director.js` rather than copied, so the figure cannot drift.
- **Her line 2 carries it**, in six words, while she is asking about something
  else: *"i can't get the door open from in here. how many of us came in."*

## 1b. The original finding, kept for the record

The owner's note that started all of this was *"I thought we were rescuing
someone, we never see her."* The agreed fix was that World 1's want is **getting
PORTER out** - his friend, the only other living person in Egypt, and the reason
he is doing her errand at all.

**None of that is in the game.** The objective ladder in `ui/objective.js` is
derived from real state and reads jars, doors, power and route cost. It says
RETURN THE JAR OF <SON>. It never says a word about her, because nothing tells it
to. So the player's stated goal for twenty-five waves is *fetch four jars*, and
the ending lands on a person they were never told they were trying to reach.

**The absence at the bottom cannot land, because nothing ever promised a
presence.** Every fragment, every line and the whole Serdab build is downstream
of a want the game does not currently express.

**Recommendation, and it is the highest-value item left in World 1:** the
objective line gains one rung above the jars - her. Not a quest marker; a stated
reason. The jars stay exactly as they are, because *doing her work while calling
it a rescue* is the structure the whole world turns on and it survives untouched.

## 8. THE MARK ON HER LAMP - CLOSED 2026-08-09, THE LINE CHANGED

**Owner's call: change the line, leave the walls alone.** Beat 4.6 above now
reads what the game does, and it is the stronger beat of the two.

The cartouche was never a wall mark. It is this game's INTERFACE grammar - every
HUD panel is cut as one, the death card is one, the pause menu is one, the last
screen in World 1 is one with four glyphs struck out of it. The player has been
reading that shape for an hour without once being able to touch it.

Putting it on her lamp is therefore the first time the shape exists as an OBJECT.
The frame he has been looking through turns out to be a thing in the room, and it
is hers. A cartouche painted on a corridor wall would have made it set dressing.

No code changed. `world/textures.js` keeps its eleven abstract marks.

Measured 2026-08-09 by `test/serdabscene.mjs` (9/9):

```
phase "none" -> "black" after 2 frame(s)
frame luminance 21.76 (spread 24.77)  ->  5.49 (spread 0.00)
```

`ui/ending.js` step() is `if (state.phase === 'none') { if (ready()) begin(); }`,
and `ready()` is true the first frame `spaces.roomId === 'serdab'`. `begin()`
raises a wash with `transition: none`. So the room the entire twenty-five-wave
siege exists to reach is on screen for two frames.

**This was correct while the room was empty and became wrong the day it was
furnished.** Nothing changed in `ending.js`; the Serdab landed underneath it.
That is the whole shape of the finding, and it is why beats 4.2 to 4.4 are
BUILT, UNSEEN rather than LIVE, and why 4.5 to 4.8 are GAPs rather than
BUILDING - there is nowhere in the sequence for them to happen.

The owner reported the symptom long before the geometry existed: *"it just turned
black. The screen turned black, and nothing happened."* `test/endgame.mjs` was
written to chase exactly that, looked at the 1.25 seconds AFTER `begin()`, and
correctly found the card healthy. It was measuring one frame too late.

**What closes it:** a fourth condition on the gate - the meeting has played - a
`src/story/meeting.js` that owns her turn and the gold telegraph in her eyes, and
a name on the archaeologist group so something can drive it.

## 2. HER LAMP, WITH THE HORDE FLOWING PAST IT (beat 3.7) is unwritten

`NARRATIVE.md` argues for this hard, and correctly: it is the ONE visible fact in
Act 3, it is the thing a second-run player goes back to find, and it is what
makes World 2's *"and they always have - you just never thought about it"* true
rather than a cheat. It is currently in no lane and no file.

It needs a map decision (a long sightline with a ledge in it) plus a scripted
lamp that the flow field ignores. Not large. Not started.

## 3. She is cut off before she has established a pattern - SOLVED BY BUILDING

Line 5 is the only line in the code. So today the beat is *a woman who has never
spoken is interrupted, and then does not speak again.* It reads as nothing. It
becomes the best beat in World 1 the moment lines 1 to 4 land, and not before.
**This is why her four lines are the cheapest high-value item on the board.**

## 4. Two introductions land on boss waves, and that is deliberate

Gold Scarab at 15 and Censer at 20 both arrive on boss waves. Checked against
`variants.js` - it is argued for, not accidental: the new enemy walks out on a
wave the player is already braced for. **Coherent. Leave it.**

## 5. The player never learns her name in play, and that is correct

PORTER appears on the briefing manifest and on the camp crates. She is never
named in dialogue, because the player character has no voice and nobody can say
it. **The player learns her name off a wall, exactly the way he learns
Hetepheres'.** That rhyme is free and should not be pointed at.

## 6. Nothing marks the grave, and nothing should

Beat 1.1. The temptation to place something at the spawn will recur every time
somebody new reads the story. `NARRATIVE.md` calls this the most protected
decision in the project. **The character does not know and is never told, and a
marker is the game leaning over and telling him.**

# WHAT THE MAP AND THE GAME OWE THIS DOCUMENT

In the owner's order - polish the paper cut first, then make the game follow it.

1. **The rescue is stated.** One rung on the objective ladder. Finding 1.
2. **Her four lines fire.** Four `speak()` calls and a room gate; the pacer, the
   codec ticks and the hold are all built and idling.
3. **The Serdab is built.** Beats 4.2 to 4.8. In flight.
4. **The four fragments deliver.** Beats 1.8, 2.8, 3.6, 3.11. In flight.
5. **Her at distance in Act 1** (beat 1.5) and **her lamp in Act 3** (beat 3.7).
6. **The camp and the wreck.** In flight.

When all six land, a player who presses BEGIN and never reads a word of
documentation gets: an order, seven names, a friend, four pieces of their own
death, a machine they charge with their colleagues, a name restored, and a room
at the bottom with the wrong person sitting in it.
