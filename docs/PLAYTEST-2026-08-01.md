# PLAYTEST 2026-08-01: a second pair of eyes

A visual sweep of the exterior courtyard and the Act 1 and Act 2 spaces, driven in a
real Chromium with real requestAnimationFrame frames and real key events. Every image
referenced here came out of `page.screenshot`, that is the browser's own compositor.
No `readPixels`, no `drawImage` on the WebGL canvas, anywhere in the capture path.

All barriers were force opened and the purse was granted 1,000,000 gold at boot, so the
sweep saw the Quarry, the Canal and the pyramid interior that a wave 1 player has not
paid for yet.

## Findings

### 1. MEDIUM. The forecourt banner hangs in mid air

- **Screenshots:**
  - `/Users/eddiebelaval/Development/sands-of-the-restless/.scratch/playtest/ext-forecourt--yaw270.png`
    (the read a player gets: the banner is the dark rectangle just left of centre, at
    roughly 40% across and 25% down, with sky above it and on both sides)
  - `/Users/eddiebelaval/Development/sands-of-the-restless/.scratch/playtest/final2-banner-under.png`
    (from underneath, the pale rectangle at the upper right, standing clear of the wall
    behind it)
  - `/Users/eddiebelaval/Development/sands-of-the-restless/.scratch/playtest/SHEET-ext-forecourt.png`
    (the full ten-view sheet; it is visible in yaw270 and yaw315 and in nothing else)
- **Taken from:** player at `(0.00, 1.80, -26.00)` in the exterior space, camera yaw 270
  degrees, that is facing +X. This is the forecourt, a few strides short of the pyramid
  doorway, on the axis every run walks down.
- **What is wrong:** the banner assembly, a cloth at
  `x 13.47..13.73, y 5.58..7.41, z -29.01..-27.99` plus a hanging rod at `y 7.40..7.46`
  and two ties, has no support and does not touch anything. It is not pinned to a wall
  face and it is not sitting on a parapet: it hovers above one.
- **Confirmed:** yes, by measurement rather than by eye. I cast a 3 by 3 grid of rays
  straight down from just under the cloth's bottom face, covering its whole footprint.
  All nine rays found the same flat top surface at `y = 4.75..5.04`, that is a clearance
  of **0.54 to 0.83 m** at every sample. A banner hung on a vertical wall face would
  have had rays miss the wall entirely; a banner resting on a parapet would have read
  zero. This one reads a uniform half metre of air under its entire plan. A ray cast
  from the player's eye through the point directly above the cloth returns no hit at
  all, that is sky.
- **Likely author:** `src/world/courtyard.js`, the `chapelSpots` push at the end of the
  alcove branch (around line 1267), consumed by `src/world/dressing.js` (the rod and tie
  meshes are parented under `dressing`). The comment at courtyard.js:1267-1270 describes
  exactly this defect and says it was fixed by making `chapelSpots` carry the bay's own
  `h`: *"the banner hung in clear sky beside a stub, a red rectangle floating over
  nothing, which is exactly the 'half rendered' read this pass is chasing out."* This
  instance is on the forecourt's east flank rather than an avenue chapel bay, so I
  believe the fix did not reach it. I am reporting it because the author's own stated
  bar says it should not be there, not because I think it was missed carelessly.
- **Confidence:** high on the geometry, high on the read from the forecourt. Note the
  fair caveat: from other angles, for instance standing at `(8, -20)` looking north
  east, the banner silhouettes against the temple mass rather than against sky, and
  looks unremarkable. It is the forecourt view that gives it away, and the forecourt is
  where the player stops.

### 2. MEDIUM. The canal barge is a hard wall down the middle of the channel

- **Screenshots:**
  - `/Users/eddiebelaval/Development/sands-of-the-restless/.scratch/playtest/confirm-canal-east-z0-before.png`
    (what the player sees at the moment they stop)
  - `/Users/eddiebelaval/Development/sands-of-the-restless/.scratch/playtest/confirm-canal-east-z0-after.png`
    (the same frame after 200 real frames of held W: unchanged)
  - `/Users/eddiebelaval/Development/sands-of-the-restless/.scratch/playtest/SHEET-canal-floor.png`
- **Taken from:** player at `(-33.00, -1.62, 0.00)`, that is standing on the canal
  channel floor, camera yaw 270 degrees, facing +X toward the east bank.
- **What is wrong:** the barge wreck's collider chain is a continuous 13 m wall along
  the channel centreline that the player cannot cross at any point, and its top stands
  0.35 m higher than the keel timber that is drawn.
- **Confirmed:** yes, by walking. Real `KeyW` keydown, 200 real frames, no `update()`
  called by hand. From `(-33, 0)` facing east the player travelled **0.40 m** and
  stopped. The same walk from `(-33, -14)`, which is clear of the wreck, carried the
  player out of the canal, through the claim gate and across to `x = +9.55`. So the
  canal is escapable, the block is the wreck.
- **The numbers.** Six colliders at `x = -31.71`, `r = 0.85`, `h = 0.9`, `y0 = -3.2`,
  spaced along z from about -10.5 to +2.5. The keel mesh they stand for is
  `slabMesh(1.1, 0.7, 13.0)` seated at `floorY + 0.2`, so the timber the player can see
  tops out at `y = -2.65` while the thing that stops them tops out at `y = -2.30`. The
  rib meshes reach `y = -1.31`, so there is real geometry above the collider too, which
  is why this reads as a defensible obstruction rather than a pure invisible wall.
- **Why it is still worth reporting.** `src/world/canal.js` states the design rule for
  this space in its own comment: *"Everything here is low: a stranded sledge timber,
  silt drifts, the ribs of a barge that was never got out. Nothing tall, because a tall
  object in a three metre trench is a corner the horde disappears behind, and the whole
  read of this space is that you can SEE what is coming down the bank at you."* The
  wreck as built stands 1.9 m off the channel floor, which is above the player's eye
  line when they are down there, and it seals 13 of the channel's 26 walkable metres
  end to end. The room's stated read does not survive its own dressing. The channel is
  still passable either side (4.2 m to the west, 2.0 m to the east), so this blocks a
  line rather than a route.
- **Likely author:** `src/world/canal.js`, the `barge` block, specifically the
  `addWallRun(midX + 1.1, barge.z, 0.85, 0.9, 'z', 13.0, floorY)` call and the rib loop
  under it.
- **REPRODUCED AT HEAD, 2026-08-02.** Same walk, clean numbers: across the wreck from
  `(-33, 0)` facing east, **0.40 m** in 200 held-W frames; the control from `(-33, -14)`,
  **16.15 m**. Not a probe artefact and not the descent.
- **ONE FIX WAS TRIED AND REVERTED. Do not repeat it.** Lowering the collider from
  `h 0.9` to `h 0.55`, the exact top of the keel timber and under the 0.65 `STEP_UP`,
  does NOT make the wreck crossable: measured 0.42 m, no better. **A collider blocks at
  any height. What you step ONTO is a deck**, and `heightAt` reads decks and ramps and
  never touches the collider list. Same mechanism as the quarry breach, whose sill step
  was drawn standable and never registered. Adding `addDeck` on the keel at
  `floorY + 0.55` did not fix it either and made it worse: the control walk fell from
  16.15 m to **0**, so that deck disturbs the channel floor somewhere it should not.
  Both reverted after isolating each against HEAD.
- **What that leaves.** The 0.35 m of collision standing above the drawn timber is a
  real and separable defect, fixable on its own. Making the wreck genuinely crossable is
  a bigger job than a height number, needs someone who works out why a deck at the
  keel's top breaks the floor beneath it, and is arguably a design call rather than a
  bug: `canal.js` says nothing tall, but a wreck you can stroll over may not be the
  wreck anybody wanted.
- **Confidence:** high on the measurements and the walk. Medium on the severity call. A
  reasonable person could say the wreck is meant to be an obstacle and this is working
  as designed; I am flagging it because the file argues the opposite in writing.

## Things I chased and dropped, with the reason

Recording these so the next person does not spend the same time on them.

- **The blue rectangle of floor under the player at the centre of the Great Gallery and
  the Hall of Offerings.** It is the Mystery Box plinth, a three tier stack topping out
  at `y 1.63`. It reads odd only because a teleport puts the camera inside it; walked
  into, it stops the player correctly. Not a defect.
- **A 3.17 by 2.2 by 3.17 mass hanging at `(-9.5, 18.35, -20.5)` with nothing under it
  for 15.9 m.** It is a colonnade capital. The supporting shaft is inside a merged batch
  whose top is at exactly `y 17.25`, the capital's underside, so my downward rays could
  not see it. Photographed and confirmed as a capital.
- **Twelve props stacked at `x ~18, y -8.2 to -3.3, z ~-149`, eight metres under the
  Granary Vault floor.** They belong to the `far-skyline` backdrop, which occupies the
  same world coordinates as the pyramid interior cell. The two spaces are never visible
  at the same time, so a player never sees them.
- **The untextured flat planks in the canal channel.** `M.palmTrunk` is a flat colour
  material with no map, used for every wooden object in the game including the palms and
  the sledge runners. Consistent, not a texture failure.
- **A purple flame in the Granary Vault.** That is the Shrine of Set, coloured per boon.
- **The blown white rectangle seen from inside the Chamber of Ascent.** That is the
  doorway daylight sheet, argued at length in `src/systems/doors.js` including the exact
  1.55 linear radiance and why it sits under the 1.60 bloom threshold. Deliberate.
- **A green shape on top of the canal's east wall.** A palm crown behind the wall, not
  an actor standing on it.

## A caveat about the negative result

I wrote a lead finder that drops a 3 by 3 grid of rays under every mesh's bounding box
and flags anything with no support anywhere under its footprint. Its first run returned
329 leads and every one was false: the rays started 2 cm **below** the bottom face, so
every prop resting on a floor plane began underneath the only surface that would have
answered. Fixed to start 0.5 m above and subtract, the interior count fell from 329 to
64 and the exterior to 18, and I checked those by hand. I mention it because it is
exactly the shape of failure this repo keeps writing comments about, and because a
reader should know the tool that produced my shortlist was wrong once before it was
right.

Two structural limits remain and I could not work around them in the time I had. First,
`batchStatics` merges most courtyard geometry, so a per-mesh support test cannot see
inside a batch and cannot distinguish a floating slab from a supported one there. The
banner in finding 1 survived that because it is dressing, built after batching. Second,
Act 3 was looked at but not reported on, per instruction.
