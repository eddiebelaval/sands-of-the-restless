# WORLDS - the three-world structure

Rewritten 2026-08-01 against `docs/story-meeting-2-2026-08-01.md`, reconciled
with meeting 1 and with four rounds of owner steering.

**This document is downstream of `docs/NARRATIVE.md`.** The story lives there,
told once, straight through. This document is the structure: what each world is
for, how the three fit together, what they cost, and what is still open. It does
not retell the plot and it should never be the place someone goes to find out
what happens.

`docs/WORLD-1.md` is downstream of both and is the buildable design for World 1.

Marker conventions are the same in all three files: plain prose is transcript or
owner decision or shipped code, **FIXED** is decided, **OPEN** carries a
recommendation, **PROPOSAL** is invented by this lane, **REVISED** means meeting
2 overturned something and the old version is named.

# WHAT MEETING 2 CHANGED

Sixteen deltas. Everything above the line is a revision to something already
written; everything below it is new material with no prior version.

## Revisions

1. **The death loop has two owners, not one.** Meeting 1 read it as the evil
   spirit raising the player. Meeting 2 adds a second, good spirit - an ancient
   gatekeeper - who stands him up because he judges him worthy and needs him.
   Both are true. She made him a corpse that walks; the gatekeeper keeps that
   corpse on its feet. See `NARRATIVE.md`, "What stands the player up".

2. **The player is not a vessel.** The old twist was that he was personally
   housing the spirit, which is why he kept rising. Meeting 2 supplies a better
   mechanism and keeps the consequence: he is part of the infected, so he goes
   through the gate with everything else. He is inventory, not a container.

3. **There is no rescue.** At 11:54: "Not rescue the girl. We find the girl."
   The dramatic question of World 1 can no longer be "who took her name and can
   we get her back". It is now "what happened here, and what am I", and the four
   jars answer it in quarters.

4. **The archaeologist IS the evil spirit and the evil spirit is a real Egyptian
   god.** Not a possessed victim who might be saved. The good one is "one of the
   ancient people", pre-Egyptian, and male throughout.

5. **The origin is concrete and it is a government's fault.** An anomaly at the
   pyramids, a government that already knew what was down there, a team of
   scientists and archaeologists sent in as test subjects under cover of
   discovery, a gate opened, a spirit out, a team dead, one hiding, one
   conscious with no memory, one bleeding out in the courtyard. Meeting 1 had
   none of this.

6. **The player died OUTSIDE the pyramid.** Confirms the pre-existing grave
   decision rather than changing it. Meeting 2 at 01:47 and the owner's separate
   "he died where we start the game" are the same fact. `(0, 30)` stays unmarked.

7. **The four jars are solved.** They are the souls of the player's own dead
   team, consumed for knowledge, and they feed a machine. This replaces "each
   jar restores one glyph of her name" as the chain's PURPOSE. The glyphs
   survive as what the player thinks he is doing.

8. **The perks are replaced by per-world enchantments.** One spell per world,
   each countering that world's special attack. World 1 swarms by numbers and
   is countered by **DOUBLE DAM**, a name the owner locked three times. World 2
   spits poisonous vomit that slows, countered by a dash that teleports through
   enemies. World 3 is the Raygun.

9. **The Raygun is ancient, not alien.** Made by the old people, in the same
   hieroglyphs as the pyramid, and the gatekeeper is one of the old people. It is
   not a weapon. It is the key that locks the gate.

10. **The ending is replaced.** The gatekeeper is freed from Area 51, gives the
    player the Raygun, and then pushes him through the gate rather than letting
    him close it, clears the witnesses, shuts the door and returns to his post.
    The betrayal is the ending. The Blockbuster is demoted from twist to coda.

11. **The world roster is named.** World 1 mummies, World 2 zombies, World 3
    aliens. Not "three floors of the same thing deeper".

12. **The geography is a three-way tunnel**, not one pyramid descended. Egypt,
    through the parallel universe, out at Area 51, which was built around a
    second gate. See "The geography, and what does not reconcile" below, because
    this is the one place the two designs genuinely collide.

13. **The tell is that the horde ignores her**, and it always has, and the player
    never noticed. Meeting 2 places this in World 2. The pre-existing tell - that
    she STOPS ASKING - is in World 1. Both survive; see the timeline note below.

14. **Meeting 1's alien experiment is gone.** The experiment was the
    government's. The aliens at the end are the audience, not the researchers.
    Straight improvement: the Blockbuster stops having to carry an explanation.

## New material with no prior version

15. **Four things meeting 2 introduces that no document had:** the flashbacks of
    the team as black silhouettes, seen while passing through the gate; the
    government infiltrated by the other side; the other realm's biome growing
    through the gate as moss or crystal; and the scientist who is pulled in and
    comes back wrong.

16. **The gatekeeper speaks.** A few words at a time, like an announcer, and he
    is the first voice in this game that is neither a system message nor the
    archaeologist.

# THE GEOGRAPHY, AND WHAT DOES NOT RECONCILE

**The two geographies are compatible in every particular except one, and the one
is worth naming plainly rather than papering over.**

## What was already written

`WORLDS.md` before meeting 2 had a single descent: three worlds going DOWN
through one pyramid, ceilings tightening from thirty units to five to under six
to none, and at the bottom the floor opens and there is no ceiling at all. The
player emerges at the POINT of a second pyramid in open space with the building
he spent three worlds inside hanging above him, inverted, and recognises it
without being told because the 62-unit stepped mass on the skyline was the first
image the game ever showed him.

## What meeting 2 says

A three-way tunnel. Egypt, into a gate at the bottom of the pyramid, out into a
parallel universe, across it to a SECOND gate, and out of that one into Area 51,
which the government built around it. Speakers 1 and 2 go back and forth on this
between 28:20 and 29:32 and it does not fully settle: at 29:12 the end of World 2
is Area 51 and World 3 is the other universe, but at 28:42 the crossing of the
parallel universe is what gets you TO Area 51, which would make the other
universe World 2 and Area 51 the seam.

## What reconciles, and it is almost all of it

The descent is not in conflict with the tunnel. A tunnel has to start somewhere,
and meeting 2 puts its mouth at the bottom of the pyramid, which is exactly where
two worlds of descent were already heading. The ceiling ladder survives. The
mummies-then-zombies roster survives. The biome leaking up through the gate
explains, for free, why the deep stone gets stranger, which the enclosure ladder
was carrying on atmosphere alone.

**The single incompatibility is apex to apex.** That beat requires the player to
come out at the bottom of the EGYPTIAN pyramid and look up at it. The three-way
tunnel routes him out at Area 51 instead, under a different desert, and the
inverted pyramid never happens.

## PROPOSAL: move the beat, do not cut it

**Put apex-to-apex at the World 2 to World 3 crossing rather than at the end.**

He goes through the gate at the bottom of the pyramid, and instead of more stone
there is a sky, and the ground under his feet is the point of a pyramid, and the
one he has been inside is hanging above him. Then he crosses the parallel
universe on foot, finds the second gate, and goes through it into Area 51.

What this keeps:

- The whole descent, unmodified, for two worlds.
- "Down becomes out", delivered exactly once, in one cut, at full strength.
- The recognition, which needs no caption and is the only image in the project
  that will never need one.
- The three-way tunnel, complete and in meeting 2's own order.
- The asset economics. It is the existing exterior mesh at a different scale and
  orientation against a sky whose entire character is seven colour uniforms and
  a sun direction.

What it costs, and it is a real cost: World 3 no longer ends on the trilogy's
best image, because it now happens two thirds of the way through.

**And the replacement is better.** Two worlds of tightening enclosure, then one
sudden sky with nothing over it, and then he walks to a door and goes back
INDOORS, into a windowless government building under fluorescent light, for the
rest of the game. The payoff arrives and is then taken away, which is what this
story does to everything else in it. The last thing that happens to him is being
put back outside through a door, permanently, by somebody who is closing it.

**Recommendation: take this.** The alternative is dropping either the inverted
pyramid or Area 51, and Area 51 is load-bearing - it is where the gatekeeper is
imprisoned, which is why the gate is unguarded, which is why there is an anomaly,
which is why anyone came. It cannot be cut. The image can move.

# THE THREE WORLDS

Purposes and structure only. The story is in `NARRATIVE.md`.

|  | World 1 | World 2 | World 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Place** | the tomb, above and inside the pyramid | the older floor beneath it, and the gate | Area 51, then the other side |
| **Enemy** | mummies | zombies | aliens, and then everything at once |
| **Special attack** | swarm by numbers | poisonous vomit that slows you | **OPEN** |
| **Enchantment** | **DOUBLE DAM** | the dash that teleports through enemies | the Raygun |
| **Architecture** | Egyptian and knowable | older than the people who named it | not architecture at all, and then a lab |
| **Ceilings** | 5 to 30 | nothing over 6 | unreadable, then none |
| **It proves** | you cannot kill a god by killing its body | you cannot kill it by killing a person | there is nothing left to kill it in |
| **Ends with** | the empty eleventh niche | being pushed through the gate | being pushed through it again |
| **Voice on the pill** | hers | hers, until she dies | his |

## The law, and the escalation that proves it

> **You cannot kill it by killing the thing it is in.**

Stated three times, at three scales, and each statement is physical rather than
spoken. World 1 proves it on a god: Set falls and does not die, it goes
somewhere. World 2 proves it on a person: the archaeologist dies and it does not.
World 3 proves why: there is nothing left for it to be in except everything.

**KILLING THE VESSEL DOES NOT REMOVE HER. IT DISTRIBUTES HER.** Take away the
thing she is in and everything becomes the thing she is in. The player caused
World 3, not as a mistake and not as a punishment but as the correct consequence
of doing what he was told, well, twice.

> **FIXED, and untouched by meeting 2.** This is the owner's own formulation and
> it is the strongest structural idea in the project after the descent. Meeting 2
> does not contradict it anywhere.

## World 1 - THE TOMB

**For: establishing that this place has rules, that they do not hold him, and
that the one person who could tell him why stops being able to.** Plus giving him
a quarter of his memory back four times.

Full buildable design in `docs/WORLD-1.md`. The three things World 2 inherits:

- **She has been taken and he does not know.** Ten waves of silence he did not
  notice.
- **He has fed four of his own colleagues into a machine** and called it
  restoring a name.
- **He has seen his own death**, from her hiding place, in the fourth fragment,
  and still does not know it was him.

The way out is the eleventh niche in the Serdab.

## World 2 - THE OLDER FLOOR, AND THE GATE

**For: taking away the rules, taking away the air, showing him the room his team
died in, and making him kill his friend.**

Deeper stone, ceilings under six, no room that gives the eye distance, and the
gods down here as carvings older than the gods. No shrine will forgive a death.

**Its design principle, which meeting 2 promoted from decoration to load-bearing:
World 2's iconography is World 1's, wrong.** The same forms, cut by someone who
had never seen the animals, in a place older than the people who named them. The
Egyptians did not invent the pantheon. They found it down here and copied it.
Never stated. Meeting 2 makes this literally true by making the old people real
and prior, and by making the good gatekeeper one of them.

**The recognition beat, staged as one object.** He finds a niche exactly like the
eleventh niche in the Serdab. It is not empty. What is in it is him.

**The crescendo. FIXED: she dies. The spirit does not.** And what makes it land
is entirely World 1's work: he is killing the person who spent five questions
trying to tell him what he was, and something took the asking away in Act 3 while
he was busy.

**Two facts, and the second is the one that matters: she stops getting up, and he
never has.**

**The twist that opens World 3.** Not "there is a floor below". It is that the
dead get worse immediately. Killing her released it rather than reducing it, and
the horde on the way out is the first evidence. He leaves World 2 having made the
problem larger by winning.

## World 3 - AREA 51, AND THE OTHER SIDE

**For: the rescue that meeting 1 promised, delivered at last and to the wrong
person. The distribution at full extension. And the betrayal.**

**PROPOSAL: still the shortest of the three.** It is the answer, and answers
should not take ten hours.

### The white room is Area 51, and that is the biggest saving in the trilogy

The pre-meeting-2 draft proposed a white featureless space at the bottom - no
wear, no dust, no tool marks, no shadow - and priced it as nearly free because it
is the SUBTRACTION of the whole material and post chain. `world/materials.js` is
warm sandstone with weathering and generated maps; `core/post.js` adds AO, height
fog, bloom, tone mapping, a grade, chromatic aberration, vignette and grain. A
white room is all of that turned off.

**A government lab built around a gate is that room and it has a reason to
exist.** Concrete, fluorescent, unworn, unshadowed, endlessly corridored,
impossible to measure yourself against. Same subtraction, same effect, and the
mystery box becomes a building the government put there, which is worse.

One honest caveat, verified in `post.js`'s own notes: the grade's shoulder is a
ceiling, grain against a ceiling clips, and bloom contributed about 300 of 1090
white-core pixels in a measured frame. So white blows out and the grain dies on
it. **That is not a problem to fix.** Three worlds of film grain and then a frame
with none is the difference between "shot on something" and "not shot at all".

### The horde, and the final boss

Everything he fights on the other side is her. The invasion and the possession
are in the same room because they are the same event, and the soldiers are not a
second faction - they are the newest thing she got into.

**HONEST FINDING, and it survives meeting 2 unchanged: a coherent alien army is
the most expensive art in the trilogy and the cheap version is the better one.**
`enemies/variants.js` opens with its own law - "a variant that is only a
different colour is not a variant... every entry here changes the OUTLINE before
it changes anything else" - so a reskinned shambler in a helmet violates the
file's stated standard.

**PROPOSAL: World 3's enemies do not agree on what they are.** If everything is
her, the correct silhouette is neither soldier nor mummy but something that
cannot commit, and the deeper in, the less any two of them resemble each other.
Cheaper than an army and truer to the distribution. The invasion is the ENGINE
and never the SUBJECT. He never fights a faction. He fights her, in an increasing
number of shapes.

**The final boss re-condenses.** Spawning stops. Every live actor stops attacking
and walks to a point. Each one that arrives is consumed and her health goes up.
She rises out of the floor as they go in.

Verified against the code: consumption is `director.retire()`, which already
frees the emitter, removes the group from the scene and swap-and-pops the live
list. And `boss.js`'s dying branch already computes the rise exactly, backwards -
it sinks by `s * spec.height * spec.scale` and scales by
`spec.scale * (1 - s * 0.5)` for `s` running 0 to 1 while `setGlow` ramps down.
**Run `s` from 1 to 0 with the glow ramping up and it is the rise.** The thing
that goes away in World 1 by sinking and going dark comes back by rising and
lighting, through the same code path.

**One UI question with a recommendation.** The boss bar reads `maxHealth`, so
during the re-condensation the player watches a health bar's maximum grow.
**Recommendation: let it, and do not hide it.** A boss bar filling itself from
the corpses of everything he just killed is the clearest possible statement of
what is happening, delivered by an element that already exists.

### And then he is thrown away

Full staging in `NARRATIVE.md`. Two structural notes belong here.

**The betrayal is not out of character, and World 1 rehearses it five times.**

> **PROPOSAL, mine.** If the old people are what got remembered as the Egyptian
> gods, then the five bosses of World 1 are the gatekeeper's colleagues, still at
> their posts. They come for the player because he came up out of the sand with
> everything else and their job is that such things are removed. Anubis weighs.
> Ammit devours the heart that fails. The death card is the verdict at the end of
> that process and the player keeps walking away from it, so the tomb sends
> something bigger.
>
> The boss ladder stops being a difficulty curve and becomes an immune response.
> And the ending is the sixth iteration of a beat the player has fought and won
> five times without once understanding what it was for.

**The ending must not be a gotcha, and the fix is the button, not a fork.**

A revelation the player cannot act on is something done to them. The pre-meeting-2
draft solved this with a choice: `ui/death.js` ends every death with a card, a
beat, and a button that arms and waits indefinitely, and the last time it appears
it means the opposite of what it has always meant.

Meeting 2 removes the choice by having the gatekeeper push him. The owner is
right that the betrayal is the ending, so do not fork it.

> **PROPOSAL: keep the button and make it the last frame.** He goes through, the
> gate shuts, and the death card comes up on the far side the way it has come up
> thirty times. `KEEP THE VIGIL [ENTER]`. He presses it, because he always has.
> Nothing stands him up, because the thing that used to do that is on the other
> side of a shut door with its back to it, and the two words that have answered
> that card since the first death do not arrive.
>
> Same DOM node, same gate, same key. He asks to come back and there is nobody
> listening. Then white, then the fizz, then the shelf.
>
> Rejected: an unwinnable boss, a cutscene death, a two-ending fork. The first
> two take the choice away and the third makes it a menu.

# THE ENCHANTMENTS

**REVISED.** This replaces perks entirely. It is the divergence from Call of Duty
Zombies that Speaker 2 asked for at 15:14, and he supplied it himself eighteen
seconds later.

| World | Enemy special | Counter | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | they swarm by numbers | **DOUBLE DAM** | **LOCKED. The owner said "lock it" three times.** |
| 2 | poisonous vomit, sticks to you, slows you | a dash that teleports you THROUGH enemies, no damage either way | **FIXED** |
| 3 | **OPEN** | the Raygun | **FIXED** |

Rules, from the room at 15:23 - 17:41:

- **One per world.** You find it, you do not buy it.
- **It fits the level.** Each world's enemies have a baseline attack and a
  special, and the world's spell counters that world's special.
- Speaker 1's framing: "whatever the peak in the chart is, your special weapon
  has to counter it".

> **PROPOSAL, mine, and it does two jobs: the enchantment survives death and
> every shrine boon does not.**
>
> `shrines.js` already drops every boon on death and the docs already read that
> as the gods withdrawing their favour from a thing that will not accept a
> verdict. If the enchantment persists through death while the gods' gifts do
> not, then the player has a permanent, visible, mechanical distinction between
> the two things resurrecting him - and it is on the HUD from the first world.
>
> Everything the gods give you is taken back every time you die. The one thing
> that stays is from the thing standing you up.

> **A collision to name: the Shrine of Set already ships "DAMAGE AND RATE UP".**
> Verified in `shrines.js`. So DOUBLE DAM must not be a shrine boon and must not
> be a floor drop, or it is a third version of something the game already has
> twice. **Recommendation: it is an ACTIVE ability with a cooldown**, which is
> the shape Speaker 1 described in the room - "a 30-second period of unlimited
> ammo and then a 90-second refresh". Passive damage is Set's. Triggered damage
> on a timer is the enchantment's, and it is a different verb.

> **Free reading, mine, offered and not insisted on.** A dam holds back a flood.
> World 1's special attack is a flood. The spell that counters a flood is a dam.
> The owner locked the name three times and may already have heard it. Nothing
> needs to be built for this and nothing should point at it.

## What the enchantments cost, verified against the code

| Piece | Status | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Timed-effect machinery | **EXISTS.** `powerups.js` has a refresh-not-stack timer, a HUD chip, and a `clearEffects()` already called on death | free |
| Grant/revoke contract | **EXISTS.** `shrines.js` boons are `apply()` plus `remove()`, capped, HUD-backed | free |
| A damage multiplier | **DOES NOT EXIST**, but there is one obvious place to put it | ~30-40 lines, and see the caveat |
| An on-demand cast with a cooldown | **DOES NOT EXIST.** `powerups.js` is a drop system, `shrines.js` is a purchase | ~40-60 lines |
| The dash itself | **DOES NOT EXIST**, but the slide is a complete template with burst speed, decel, max time and cooldown already tuned | ~80-120 lines |
| Dashing THROUGH enemies | **FREE.** Player-versus-enemy collision does not exist. No enemy module ever pushes to `world.colliders`. The player already walks through them | 0 lines |
| No damage during the dash | **EXISTS.** `combat.state.invulnerable` gates every damage path including boss projectiles and shockwaves, and is documented as unused by the game | ~2 lines |
| The poison slow the dash counters | **DOES NOT EXIST.** There is no status-effect system on the player at all - no slow, no DoT, no stack, no duration | ~100 lines, and it is the risky half |

**The caveat on the damage multiplier, and it matters.** Bullet damage is
computed in exactly one line, but grenade damage arrives at the combat system
already computed by `grenades.js`, and melee is a third path. A DOUBLE DAM that
silently excludes grenades and the khopesh is a bug report waiting to happen.
Doing it honestly means applying the multiplier at the three points where an
enemy is actually hurt rather than at the weapon-stats lookup.

**The load-bearing finding: the poison is more expensive than the dash.** World 2
needs a player status-effect system that does not exist anywhere, and the one
place a speed change can safely land is complicated by the fact that the existing
speed perk is implemented as a second player update with a shorter delta
specifically to avoid breaking collision. Slowing is the safe direction, which
helps. Budget it as World 2's real engineering item.

# THE RAYGUN, AND THE SUNSPEAR

> **OPEN, with a recommendation.**

The game already ships an energy rifle called the Sunspear, which the owner calls
"Raygun-ish". It is mystery-box exclusive and it is also the intended reward for
World 1's four-jar chain.

**Recommendation: keep both, and make the Sunspear the Raygun's lesser
ancestor.** Do not replace it, do not rename it, do not build the Raygun as a
re-skin of it. The Sunspear is a small old-people tool left in a sealed chapel.
The Raygun is the one that locks the gate. Same makers, same script, same light.

What it buys: the player has been carrying a piece of the ending since World 1
and using it to shoot mummies with, and when the gatekeeper hands him the Raygun
in World 3 he is holding a bigger version of a thing he found in her serdab.

**Cost of the Raygun, verified.** Six or seven files, roughly 200 to 280 lines,
two or three test files, no architecture change. The weapon table is genuinely
data-driven but it is five to seven tables across four files, and one of them is
geometry: the dominant cost is 130 to 200 lines of hand-authored procedural
viewmodel geometry, measured against its peers in the same file. One hard rule:
the weapon slot array IS the number-key binding, so a new weapon is **appended**,
never inserted.

# THE VOICES, AND WHO OWNS WHICH SURFACE

**PROPOSAL, mine, and it is the cheapest way to keep two supernatural powers
distinguishable without a word of exposition.**

| | Surface | Register |
|---|---|---|
| **The archaeologist, and the thing wearing her** | the notice pill | lowercase, the only lowercase in a game of capitals |
| **The gatekeeper** | the death card | two or three words, never a sentence |
| **Everything else** | the notice pill, the objective panel, the prompt | capitals, systemic, derived |

**They never share a surface.** That is the mechanical statement that they are
different powers, and the player absorbs it without ever being told there are
two.

**And the channel is inherited.** She owns the pill for all of World 1 and the
first half of World 2. She dies. From that point the pill is his, and the game
has a new voice in an old place, which is the only announcement the handover
needs.

## What each costs, verified

**The gatekeeper on the death card: near free.** `ui/death.js` already stops the
world, draws a cartouche with a verdict in it and a line beneath, holds a beat,
and arms a button. Two words in a third slot is one span and a string table. This
is the cheapest strong beat available in the entire project.

**Her lowercase in the pill: small.** The notice function is seven lines. It
already overwrites in place with no transition at all, which is what makes the
Kindling beat work. What she needs is a hold - a system notice arriving during
one of her lines is dropped rather than deferred - which is one boolean, one
timestamp, and one deliberate exception.

**An in-run announcer queue: DOES NOT EXIST and should not be built for World
1.** There is no queue anywhere in the UI. Every text channel is last-write-wins
or highest-priority-wins. A real "few words at a time" queue is roughly 80 to 120
lines built on the existing priority bus. **Recommendation: build it in World 2,
when she is dead and the pill is free.** In World 1 the gatekeeper speaks on the
death card only, which is the surface she can never contest.

**Voice audio is not achievable.** The audio engine is 1,875 lines of synthesised
WebAudio with no sample-playback path at all - no decode, no mp3, no ogg, no wav
anywhere in the project. "Voice" in that file means a synth voice. Adding spoken
lines is a real architectural addition to a file whose entire premise is that
everything is generated. **All three voices are text.**

# THE FLASHBACKS, PRICED

Meeting 2 asks for a cut scene at the gate showing the science team as black
silhouettes. That is a real new requirement and it is priced here honestly rather
than assumed away.

## What a cut scene actually costs

| Piece | Status | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Fade to black and back | **EXISTS**, but private to the space router, which deliberately exposes a read-only copy so nobody can strand the player in the dark | expose two methods, ~20 lines |
| Input lock | **EXISTS**, and it clears as well as gates, which is the difference between a lock and a flag | 1 line |
| Simulation freeze | **EXISTS**, and there is already a precedent for exempting one lane from it | ~5 lines |
| Letterbox | **DOES NOT EXIST** anywhere | ~15 lines of DOM |
| A sequence driver | **DOES NOT EXIST.** The death card's three-phase state machine is the template | ~60 lines |
| Silhouette figures | **the geometry EXISTS.** There is a humanoid accumulator built specifically so that limb taper reads as a body rather than a mannequin, and an unlit black material is one line plus a tone-mapping opt-out | ~100-150 lines |
| **A camera move** | **THIS IS THE PROBLEM.** The camera is welded to the player's head. The only authored camera move in the game is the death fall, and it is additive-only - it can pitch, yaw, roll and drop the view, but it cannot detach it | 80-150 lines, and every consumer reads through that rig |

**Total for a real cut scene: one substantial new module of roughly 300 to 450
lines plus surgical edits to three files, and the load-bearing risk is the camera
rig, which is written on the assumption that the camera IS the player.**

## PROPOSAL: put the flashbacks on the jars and there is no cut scene

Four jars, four fragments, delivered where the player already stops and
interacts, spread across a whole world instead of dumped in one place. The fourth
completes the picture and it lands in the deepest room of World 1. The gate
crossing at the end of World 2 then needs no scripted sequence at all, because the
player has known what happened since the fourth jar.

**A fragment is: freeze, black, a still tableau of unlit black humanoids at a
fixed distance in front of a camera that never moves, three seconds, out.**

| Piece | Cost |
|---|---|
| Curtain exposure | ~20 |
| Input lock and sim freeze | ~6 |
| Sequence driver | ~60 |
| Silhouette figures and a posed rig | ~100-150 |
| **Camera move** | **0. The camera never detaches, so the expensive item is not bought at all.** |

**Roughly 190 to 240 lines, no letterbox, no camera work, no new art assets,
because the humanoid geometry is procedural and already in the project.** About
half a cut scene, delivered four times instead of once, in the world that ships
first.

And it reads better. A held tableau with no camera is what a MEMORY looks like. A
moving camera is what a SCENE looks like, and he is not remembering a scene. He is
remembering four seconds at a time, from four dead people's positions.

> **OPEN, and it is a taste call rather than an engineering one.** The
> alternative is 2D: draw the silhouettes as a flat overlay on the curtain, which
> costs about 60 lines and four hand-made images and no geometry at all.
> **Recommendation: 3D, using the existing humanoid accumulator.** The project has
> no hand-made image assets anywhere and introducing one pipeline for four
> pictures is a worse trade than 150 lines of procedural bodies.

# THE TIMELINE PROBLEM, AND ITS FIX

Meeting 2 puts the horde's indifference to the archaeologist in World 2, and
Speaker 1 lands the line that makes it work: "and they always have. But you just
never thought about it."

**That claim is only true if the player COULD have seen it earlier**, and in
World 1 as designed she is never in the same frame as the horde. She is a lamp
ahead in the dark and a voice, and she does not have a body until the Serdab. So
a replaying player has nothing to go back and find, and the best line in meeting 2
is a claim about a thing that never happened.

> **PROPOSAL: show it once in Act 3 of World 1, after she is taken, unremarked.**
>
> Down a long sightline, her lamp is standing still and the horde is flowing
> around it. Not away from it and not toward it: past it, the way water goes past
> a rock, while the player is fighting for his life in the foreground.
>
> It does not contradict "she is herself until the Kindling", because it happens
> after. It gives Act 3 the one visible fact it currently lacks - the design's own
> instruction for those ten waves is that nothing visibly changes, which is
> correct for the absence device and leaves the act with nothing to show. And it
> means that when the player finally SEES it in World 2, a second run can go back
> and find the exact wave it started.
>
> **Both tells survive and they are doing different jobs.** The asking stops in
> World 1 and it is a removal nobody notices. The horde's indifference is what the
> player finally SEES in World 2, and it retroactively explains a thing they
> already had on screen.

# WHAT MERESANKH BECOMES

The full argument is in `NARRATIVE.md` under "The two decisions waiting on the
owner". The short version, because this is the file people will check:

**Recommendation: she stays as a NAME and stops being a character.** Meresankh is
the dead queen whose tomb this is. Restoring her effaced cartouche is what an
archaeologist would actually be doing down here and it is what the player thinks
he is doing while he charges a machine with four of his colleagues. Nobody
rescues her. The antagonist is nameless in World 1 - a sign, not a noun.

**If the owner wants the antagonist to literally be a named Egyptian goddess, as
meeting 2 says: NEITH, and not until World 2.** She is genuinely pre-dynastic and
therefore older than most of the pantheon, she is a guardian of the dead, she is
in neither the boss roster nor the shrine list so there is no collision, and she
is already wired into this map as one of the four goddesses standing behind the
four canopic sons. Which would mean the player hands his four dead colleagues
into her custody, one at a time, personally.

# THE UNIFICATION

The gate at the bottom of the pyramid, the second gate at Area 51, and the alien
video store are the same route.

- The other universe is where the aliens live.
- The game closes in an alien video store.
- Therefore the door he is pushed through at the end opens onto the shelf.

He has been walking toward that shelf since the first wave, which is to say since
before the first wave, on a walk down an avenue that killed him.

And it makes the last exchange of meeting 1 pay twice:

> "And for them we're the aliens." / "Yeah, exactly, we are the aliens."

The aliens have been trying to come through into our world. He goes OUT through
it, and he is the thing coming through from the other side, and he is not going
back.

# STRUCTURAL FINDINGS THAT AFFECT ALL THREE WORLDS

Verified against the code. These are findings, not permission to build.

**1. World 2 is structurally the CHEAPEST thing in this document, and that is the
surprising result.** The interior room graph is pure data with no geometry and no
Three.js import, and the builder already takes the room list as a parameter with
the shipped graph as its default. A second room graph passed to the same builder
produces a second interior with zero changes to the builder. The work is: author
the data, generalise the two-valued space enum into a keyed registry, move the
hardcoded interior bounds onto the graph record, and make the entry point
per-world. Roughly 90 to 150 lines of engineering, and everything else is design
effort. **No renderer work, no loader, no asset pipeline.**

**2. Both worlds are resident in memory at boot and nothing is ever disposed.**
The space router toggles visibility on subtrees; no geometry is ever freed. The
interior is described in its own comment as costing "half a megabyte of
geometry". **This is the real constraint on the owner's "we need more space", and
it compounds when World 2 lands.** The map lane should know it before it starts.

**3. The Serdab is currently unreachable.** Its portal is the only puzzle-gated
door in the game and it reads a jar counter that is hardcoded to zero and never
written. The room where World 1's entire ending happens cannot be entered in the
shipped build.

**4. The jar system does not exist in any form.** Four jars are placed and have
meshes, four niches are placed and have meshes, both are published to the rest of
the game, and nothing reads either. There is no pickup, no carry, no deposit, no
counter, no completion. There is also no carried-item primitive anywhere in the
codebase to copy - the nearest analogue is proximity-collected floor drops.

**5. The game has no concept of a run ending.** The boss roster cycles forever
and nothing in the main loop or the wave director can conclude a run. This is
correct for an endless build and wrong for a world with an ending, and it is
World 1's largest engine gap.

**6. There is no player status-effect system.** No slow, no poison, no
damage-over-time, no duration on the player at all. World 2's poison needs one
built from nothing.

**7. Ordinary enemies have no ranged attack.** Projectiles exist, pooled and
gravity-arced, but only on bosses. World 2's vomit is either a boss-only
mechanic, or the projectile pool is generalised down to variants.

**8. There is no character model for the player and there never has been.** The
viewmodel is first-person hands and a weapon, rendered through its own camera in
its own pass. The only time the player sees himself is the box art at the end.

# OPEN ACROSS ALL THREE

Each is a question with a recommendation, not a menu.

1. **Is Meresankh a name on a wall, or the antagonist?** **Recommendation: a name
   on a wall, and the antagonist stays nameless until World 2.** This is one of
   the two decisions genuinely waiting on the owner.

2. **Is the Kindling rewritten?** **Recommendation: repointed, not rewritten.**
   Full cost in `docs/WORLD-1.md`. This is the other one.

3. **Does he ever learn what he is, on screen?** **Recommendation: no explicit
   statement.** He learns it by being thrown through a door by somebody who is
   sorting the infected from everything else, and by nothing arriving when he
   asks to come back.

4. **Does he ever learn she watched him die?** **Recommendation: yes, once, and
   he does not recognise it.** The fourth fragment is his own death seen from her
   hiding place. The player works out whose eyes those are, or does not, and both
   are acceptable outcomes for a first run.

5. **Is the depth readout carried across all three worlds?** **Recommendation:
   yes, and in World 3 it is the last instrument still reporting anything.** The
   owner has now also asked for the descent to shape the map, which supersedes
   the earlier recommendation to leave the geometry horizontal. See the map brief
   in `WORLD-1.md`.

6. **How long is World 2?** **Recommendation: shorter than World 1.** One
   recognition and one killing.

7. **Do the gods appear outside World 1?** **Recommendation: as carvings in World
   2, never as bosses.** They are the gatekeeper's colleagues and they are still
   in Egypt at their posts.

8. **Does the horde in World 3 include shapes from World 1?** **Recommendation:
   yes, and it should be the first thing he notices.** A shambler in a government
   corridor is the distribution stated in one silhouette.

9. **Does she speak again after World 1?** **Recommendation: once.** Not as
   herself. As the thing wearing her, using her voice, in lowercase, saying
   something that is unmistakably not a question. It is the only way to make the
   player certain about what they half-noticed in Act 3, and it should cost her
   life within the minute.

10. **What is World 3's enemy special attack?** **Genuinely open and the room
    never got to it.** **Recommendation: they do not have one, and the Raygun is
    not a counter to anything.** World 3's whole point is that the rules stop
    applying, and a third neat attack-and-counter pair would be the game
    insisting it still has a system when it should be losing one. The Raygun is a
    key that happens to kill things.

11. **Is there a World 4 hook?** **Recommendation: the shelf is full of other
    boxes and none of them are legible.** A named sequel turns an ending into an
    advertisement.
