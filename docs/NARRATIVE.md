`docs/story-meeting-2-2026-08-01.md` (meeting 2), reconciled. This is the story,

# PART ZERO - BEFORE THE GAME

None of this is playable. About a third of the story happens before the title
screen and the player learns it in quarters, out of order, from four jars.
Writing it down first is the only way the four fragments can be authored to add
up.

## The old people, and what they were guarding

Before Egypt there were people here, and they built the gate.

They did not build it to open. They built it because it was already there, and
what they built around it was a lock and a post and a rule. There is another
universe on the far side and the two have been at war for as long as either has
been able to reach the other. The old people's job was that nothing crossed.

> **FIXED (meeting 2, 08:42 and 22:31).** "Maybe the Egyptian gods were the
> gatekeepers of this universe and stopped the other universe from breaking
> through, because they've been at war. So the Egyptian gods were real." And:
> "No, the spirit is one of the old people." / "One of the ancient people."

> **PROPOSAL, mine, and it reconciles those two lines rather than choosing
> between them.** The old people are what got remembered as the Egyptian gods.
> The Egyptians did not invent the pantheon. They came later, found the works of
> people who had been here first, and copied them, and the names we use now -
> Anubis, Ammit, Apep, Sekhmet, Set - are Egyptian names for something older
> that was already standing at its post. So both meeting-2 statements hold: the
> Egyptian gods were real gatekeepers, AND the good spirit is one of the ancient
> people. Same class of being, two names, and the older name is the true one.
>
> This was already proposed in `WORLDS.md` as World 2's design principle before
> meeting 2 happened. Meeting 2 promoted it from decoration to load-bearing.

The old people made tools for the job. The tools are cut with the same
hieroglyphs as the pyramid, because the pyramid is theirs, and the Egyptians
copied the writing too. One of those tools is the Raygun, and it is not a
weapon. It is the key that locks the gate.

One of the old people was left on watch. He is the gatekeeper. He has been at
his post since before there was an Egypt, he does not age, and standing next to
that door for that long has done something to him: he has been morphing into
something that is no longer entirely one of the old people either.

> **FIXED (meeting 2, 22:20 - 23:03).** The old people made the gun. The spirit
> is one of them. He is "the ancient gatekeeper tasked with staying around and
> keeping track of everything", and he "got an effect from the gate. That's why
> he started morphing."

## The Egyptians, and what a pyramid is for

The Egyptians built on top. They built the tombs, they buried their dead under
the sand in every direction, and they put a queen's chapel deep in the structure
with a serdab in it, which is the sealed room where the part of a person that is
not the body is kept.

They did not know what was underneath them, or they knew it as a religion. Both
readings are available and the game never settles it.

> **PROPOSAL, mine.** The queen is MERESANKH, a real Fourth Dynasty queen buried
> at Giza whose tomb's famous feature is its serdab, with a row of women carved
> into the wall. She is dead and she is not a character. Her name is on a
> cartouche in the serdab and somebody struck it out. See "What happened to
> Meresankh" at the end of this document, because meeting 2 changed what her
> name is FOR and the change is worth reading on its own.

## The anomaly

In the present, a government instrument reads something at Giza that should not
be there. A gravitational pull, or a magnetic one, in a radius around the
pyramid. Something measurable, difficult to read, and wrong.

> **FIXED (meeting 2, 00:25 - 00:42).** "The government, or CIA, started reading
> a different kind of magnetic pull, or gravity-ish, something they could
> measure. They measured something at the pyramids that seemed off."

The anomaly has a cause and the cause is that the gate is no longer guarded.

## Why it is no longer guarded

The government got there first, years earlier. When they first entered the
pyramid they found a man standing in front of a door, and the man was not a man.
They took him. He is in Area 51 now, in a chamber built to hold him, and Area 51
itself was built around a second gate they had already found on their own soil.

Removing the gatekeeper is what started the anomaly. The pull the instruments
picked up is the door leaning open.

> **FIXED (meeting 2, 23:03 - 23:42 and 28:32).** "Government came. When they
> first entered the pyramids they found him guarding the gate. Government
> captured him and took him to Area 51. That means the gate was not protected
> anymore, because the gatekeeper is gone." And: "Area 51 was built because they
> found a gate there. That's a whole lab that was actually just built around the
> gate."

## What the government knew, and what it sent

The government knew exactly what was down there. It had the gatekeeper in a cell
and it had a second gate of its own to study. What it did not know was what the
gate would do to a person.

So it sent people to find out, under cover of discovery. A team of scientists
and archaeologists, briefed to investigate an anomaly, actually dispatched as
test subjects. The cover was real enough that the team believed it. The
instruction was to reach the anomaly and open what was in front of it.

> **FIXED (meeting 2, 00:36 - 00:55 and 09:06 - 10:33).** "They sent us as
> scientists, or a group of scientists and archaeologists... to go inside and
> discover what's going on." And: "They secretly knew what it was. They were
> just sending scientists and archaeologists as test subjects to see what it
> would do with a person."

There is a second reason the government behaves the way it does and the story
only states it at the very end, if at all. The other side has put people inside
it. The reason a government with a captured gatekeeper and a known gate keeps
pushing at both is that some of the people making the decisions want the door
open.

> **FIXED as material (meeting 2, 09:20 - 10:05).** "The other universe sent
> spies and implanted them in the government to try to get us to let them
> through." Speaker 1 immediately deprioritised it himself: "maybe that could be
> something further, deeper, where we reveal that in the end. It's just another
> thread."
>
> **PROPOSAL, mine: it is never stated in dialogue and never confirmed.** It
> exists as the answer to a question the player will ask on their own - why did
> they send us at all - and the game's answer is that it does not answer. Keep
> it in the world so the set dressing can be consistent with it. Do not build a
> beat on it. If it is ever paid, it is paid in World 3 inside Area 51, where
> the evidence would physically be.

Among them: an archaeologist, and the man the player will be. They know each

It is not one of the old people and it is not from here. It came through, and

direction, and there are thousands, and that is what the pyramids are full of.
The dead get up.

> **FIXED (meeting 2, 01:09 - 01:32).** "The soul, the spirit, came, killed

It is female throughout both meetings. It has no name of its own. It takes them.

## What stands the player up

He is one of the dead. The spirit raised everything under that sand and he was
lying on top of it.

But he gets up conscious. Every other risen thing in Egypt gets up savage, and
he gets up with his mind intact and no memory of anything, and that is the
anomaly inside the anomaly.

> **FIXED (meeting 2, 01:32 - 01:45).** "But somehow, for some reason, we, the
> main character, awake, but not in a savage-like mean air; still conscious, but
> we don't know anything." Speaker 2 explicitly deferred the reason: "that
> reason will be revealed some other way."

The reason is the gatekeeper. In a cell under a desert on the other side of the
world, a thing that has been at a post since before Egypt is watching the only
mind that came back up out of that horde, and it decides that this one is
worthy, and it starts standing him up.

> **FIXED (meeting 2, 02:24 - 03:46).** "There has to be a reason why we keep
> coming back to life." / "It's not the player, but it's just helping us because
> it knows that we are worthy." / "It just says, you know what, then on repeat,
> we're going to play this on repeat until I can guide you all the way to the
> end, because I need you to shut down the door."

**This is the death loop, and it has two owners.** The spirit made him a corpse
that walks. The gatekeeper stands that corpse up after every single death,
because he needs it to reach the bottom. Neither of them tells him.

> **This is the reconciliation the whole document turns on, so here it is
> explicitly, with the reasoning.**
>
> Meeting 1 read the loop as the evil spirit raising the player. Meeting 2 read
> it as the good spirit resurrecting him because he is worthy. **Both are true
> and they are doing different jobs.**
>
> | | The spirit | The gatekeeper |
> |---|---|---|
> | What it did | raised everything under the sand, including his corpse | picked one of the risen and kept it standing |
> | Scale | every dead thing in Egypt | exactly one man |
> | The player experiences it as | the condition of being what he is | getting back up after each death |
> | Tells him | nothing, ever | two or three words at a time |
>
> **The test the reconciliation has to pass is the ending, and it passes.** The
> gatekeeper throws him through the gate at the end for being what the OTHER one
> made him. He is part of the infected. The gate closes on everything that came
> through, and he is on the list, and the being that has been standing him up for
> three worlds is the one holding the list. If the two powers were the same
> power, the ending is a villain reveal. Because they are two, the ending is a
> man being correctly categorised by somebody who was never lying to him.

> **REVISED, and the old version has to go.** `WORLD-1.md` and `WORLDS.md` were
> written on meeting 1 only, where the loop had ONE owner and the twist was that
> the player is a vessel carrying the spirit, which is why he keeps rising, and
> the ending was that he chooses to stay down. Meeting 2 replaces the mechanism.
> **What survives from the old version:** he IS one of the risen, he IS part of
> her work, and killing the thing means he goes with it. Meeting 2 says this
> outright at 31:24 - "now that we're dead, we're also basically part of the
> gate, part of the infected." So the twist was right and the plumbing was
> wrong. **What is discarded:** the reading where the player is personally
> housing the spirit the way the archaeologist is. He is not a vessel. He is
> inventory.

> **OPEN, and the room genuinely disagrees with itself here.** At 31:37 Speaker
> 2 says "He got us infected as well to help him", which would mean the
> GATEKEEPER arranged the player's infection.
>
> **Recommendation: reject it.** Three reasons. It makes the gatekeeper a plain
> villain and collapses the ending into "he was evil all along", which is the
> weakest available version and which the design has already rejected once. It
> contradicts the timeline, because the spirit was loose and raising everything
> before the player finished bleeding out, so no special intervention was needed.
> And the sentence is ambiguous in speech: "he got us infected to help him" reads
> equally as "he made use of our being infected", which is what everything else
> in the scene supports.
>
> **What to keep from it:** the gatekeeper CHOSE. He did not make the player
> dead, but he looked at a field of risen corpses and selected one and has been
> maintaining it ever since. That is enough to make "worthy" true and enough to
> make the discard hurt.

## How the gatekeeper talks

A few words at a time. Never a sentence. Something between an announcer and a
voice in the back of the head, and it arrives at moments the player cannot
predict.

> **FIXED (meeting 2, 08:23).** "We hear a voice in our head every time, or like
> an announcer. But it's only like a couple words, not full sentences." Speaker
> 2 then names it: "that is secretly the god that is resurrecting us every time."

> **PROPOSAL, mine, and it costs almost nothing: he speaks on the death card and
> nowhere else.** The game already stops the world on death, draws a cartouche
> with UNWORTHY in it and THE HEART OUTWEIGHS THE FEATHER beneath, holds a beat,
> and arms a button. That is a verdict being passed, delivered on average twenty
> times a run, and it is the most-read text in the game. Two words underneath it,
> in a different treatment, disagreeing with the verdict, is the entire
> characterisation of the gatekeeper delivered for the price of one span.
>
> It also gives the two supernatural powers **separate surfaces, which is the
> mechanical statement that they are different powers.** She and the things
> wearing her get the notice pill. He gets the death card. They never share.

# WORLD 1 - THE TOMB

**The player is outside the pyramid, on the spot where he died, and the game
begins.**

> **WORLD BOUNDARY.** World 1 runs from the first frame in the courtyard to the
> moment the player climbs into the eleventh niche in the Serdab. It is
> complete, shippable and comprehensible on its own. A player who finishes World
> 1 and never plays another minute has had a whole story: a tomb, a job, a
> friend, a name, and a room at the bottom with the wrong person sitting in it.

## What World 1 is for

Three things, and everything in it serves one of them.

1. **Establish that this place has rules, that the rules do not hold him, and
   that the one person who could tell him why stops being able to.**
2. **Give him a quarter of his memory back four times**, so that the player
   learns the origin in Part Zero in fragments, in the order the map hands them
   over rather than the order they happened.
3. **Spend nothing.** Everything about the gate, the government, the other side
   and the gatekeeper's imprisonment is planted here and confirmed later. World
   1 is the last time the world looks knowable and that belief is the asset the
   other two worlds spend.

## Act 1 - outside

He is standing in the courtyard, in the open, on the avenue, facing the pyramid.
There is a modern expedition camp out here with nobody in it. The generators are
running, the tents are up, the crates are stencilled, and there is an instrument
array still logging an anomaly that nobody is reading.

> **PROPOSAL, mine, and it is the largest new SPACE requirement in World 1.**
> The camp does not exist in the map today. It is now mandatory, because it is
> the only place in the entire trilogy where the human institution that caused
> all of this is physically present before the player reaches Area 51 in World
> 3. It is also where the game establishes, without one line of text, that
> people came here on purpose with equipment and are not here now.

The dead are up and they are coming across the sand. He kills them and takes
their gold, because the tomb's gold is the only currency in this world and he
has no idea why that feels normal to him.

There is a doorway into the pyramid and it is blocked. He came out of it four
days ago at a dead run, bleeding, and something came out behind him. He does not
remember that. The first thing the player buys in this game is the way back into
the building he died escaping.

> **REVISED.** `WORLD-1.md` currently says the sealed doorway is "the thing he
> died in front of and never got through". Meeting 2 makes that wrong: he came
> OUT through it. The correct line is that the first purchase in the game is
> going back in, and it costs a thousand gold he does not have, so he has to earn
> it off the dead first.

In a side chapel on the walk down the avenue there is a canopic jar. It is the
first of four and it is already placed in the shipped map.

**He takes it, and something happens that has never happened to him.** He is
somewhere else, briefly. Black shapes in a stone room, seven of them, doing
something to a door. No faces, no sound he can place, no idea who any of them
are. Then he is holding a jar in a chapel in the sun.

> **FIXED as content (meeting 2, 06:46 - 07:56).** "We show little visions of
> who we really are as a person, because we don't know. But secretly we do. It's
> just in the back of our head where we don't reach." And: "Like a black
> silhouette almost, of us, the whole group of scientists who open the door. But
> they're a black silhouette, so they don't know who we are. Then we show how we
> died... it shows how we open the door, how we got attacked from zombies, and
> how we die." / "That we died and she was there."

> **PROPOSAL, mine, and it is the single biggest cost saving available in either
> meeting.** Meeting 2 asks for a CUT SCENE at the gate, in World 2, delivering
> all of this at once. A cut scene is technology this game does not have and the
> design has refused twice on principle.
>
> **Move the flashbacks onto the jars and there is no cut scene.** Four jars,
> four fragments, delivered where the player already stops and interacts, spread
> across a whole world instead of dumped in one place. The fourth one completes
> the picture and it lands in the deepest room in World 1. The gate crossing at
> the end of World 2 then needs no scripted sequence at all, because the player
> already knows what happened and has known since the fourth jar.
>
> The four jars hold his four dead colleagues. Consuming a soul for what it knew
> is meeting 2's own mechanic, said in the room and not softened: "The jars are
> the souls of the scientists." / "We consume the jars to give us the knowledge."
> The knowledge they give him is what they saw. He is eating his own team to
> remember his own death.
>
> **What a fragment can actually be, priced honestly:** the game has a curtain
> that holds at full black for two drawn frames, a post chain with a grade and a
> vignette, an unlit material path, and no character models. So a fragment is a
> few seconds of held frame with unlit black shapes on it and no animation. That
> is exactly the "black silhouette" meeting 2 asked for, and the reason it works
> is that silhouettes are what you get when you cannot afford characters and also
> what the scene requires. Cost is real but it is not a cut scene: no camera
> path, no choreography, no input lock beyond what the death card already does.

## Act 1, the other thing that happens: she calls out

There is a woman ahead of him with a lamp.

She is the archaeologist. She hid, she survived, she has been down in this
building for four days doing her work in the dark because it is the only thing
she has, and she has just seen a man she watched die walk up the avenue.

She calls to him. He does not answer, because the player has no voice, which is
so ordinary in a first-person game that nobody notices it is happening. To her
it is a man who will not speak.

She asks him a question. It is not "how are you alive", because that question is
unaskable. It is around it, and it is her checking whether SHE is the one who is
wrong.

> "i keep thinking you were further back. that walk in."

> **FIXED as a device (owner, and preserved in full in `WORLD-1.md`).** She asks
> five times across World 1, in five different rooms, in a recognisable shape,
> always offering him the chance to correct her memory, and he never takes it.
> She is the only lowercase text in a game where every other character on screen
> is a capital, and that is her entire attribution: no name tag, no portrait, no
> new UI element.

## Act 2 - inside, and the survey

The doorway opens and the building is bigger inside than it is outside. Nobody
mentions it. It is simply true, it has been true in the shipped build for weeks
for engineering reasons, and no line of copy is ever allowed to excuse it.

He goes through rooms full of somebody's work. Chalk numbers on columns. A
folding stool. Survey pegs. Rope strung as a handline where a room is dangerous.
A notebook. The work gets fresher the deeper he goes, and the lamps get warmer,
because he is following a living person and reading it as archaeology.

> **FIXED as staging (`WORLD-1.md`, the camp gradient).** Bleached in Act 1,
> crisp in Act 2, warm in Act 3, and nothing modern at all in the boss room,
> because whoever left the trail either never went in there or never came out.

She asks him twice more. Once just inside, counting the crew and getting a
number she cannot make work. Once in the biggest and loudest room in the map,
from a ledge six metres up, in the middle of a fight, where she starts to say
something about him and stops herself, and the line does not finish, and it goes
by.

The second jar is up a shaft that points at the sky. There is a rope in it that
goes up into the dark and ends at nothing, because she went looking for a way
out that way and there is not one. He takes the jar and he is somewhere else
again: the same seven black shapes, and now there is growth on the wall behind
them in a colour that is not a colour, and one of the shapes is standing much too
close to the door.

## Act 3 - the machine, the silence, and the name

Deeper. The rooms get lower. The last room in the world has the lowest ceiling
in the game and it is where he leaves from, which nobody planned.

There is a room down here with four empty niches in it, one for each son of
Horus, and a notebook open on a table with the four niches chalk-numbered in the
wrong order. She tried this and got it wrong. She is an archaeologist doing her
job badly, under pressure, in the dark, with something killing her friends.
There is nothing sinister in that room. That is what makes it hers.

He puts the jars in the niches. Third jar, third fragment: the shapes are being
killed now, and one of them is him, and the perspective is wrong because he is
watching himself from where she was hiding.

**And the third jar going home turns the building on.**

Not a lever. Not a switch he walks to. The machine is the four niches and what
it runs on is his team. Light comes up across nine rooms, a horn sounds and then
a chime two hundred and sixty milliseconds later, six dead shrines wake, a gate
opens deeper in, and a notice fires that says THE KINDLING TAKES - THE PYRAMID
WAKES.

> recommendation are in `docs/WORLD-1.md`.** The Kindling ships today as a fire
> you light in a dead-end room, and Speaker 2 asked to get rid of it because the
> power-then-perks-then-pack-a-punch chain is Call of Duty Zombies. Speaker 1
> noted it is already coded.
>
> **Recommendation: keep every line of the power system and move only its
> trigger.** What Speaker 2 objected to at 15:05 is the CHAIN, not the object,

# THE TWO DECISIONS WAITING ON THE OWNER

## 1. What happened to Meresankh

**The problem.** `WORLD-1.md` is built on a rescue: the player reassembles the
name MERESANKH glyph by glyph and the dramatic question of World 1 is "what was
her name and who took it". Meeting 2 says two things that break it. At 11:54:
"Not rescue the girl. We find the girl." And at 02:42 the archaeologist IS the
evil spirit and the evil spirit is "a real Egyptian god", while the good one is
one of the ancient people. Meresankh is a real queen, not a god, and there is no
longer anyone to rescue.

She was chosen for good reasons that have not gone away: her tomb's defining
feature is a serdab, the map has an unused Serdab, her name means roughly "she
loves life" which is the diagnosis of a thing that will not let anything stay
dead, and she was buried in a sarcophagus originally cut for her mother, which is
a person in a vessel that was not hers.

**RECOMMENDATION: she stays, demoted from spirit to NAME.**

Meresankh is the dead queen whose tomb this is. She is not a character, not an
antagonist, and nobody rescues her. Her name is what the struck cartouche in the
serdab says, restoring an effaced royal name is a real thing archaeologists
actually do, and it is what the archaeologist was down here doing when everything
went wrong. It is a completely mundane, completely correct motivation that needs
no supernatural justification at all.

What that buys:

- **The player's errand keeps its cover story and gains its real one.** He thinks
  he is finishing his friend's academic work. He is actually charging a machine
  with four of his colleagues. Both are true, the game never winks, and
  `WORLD-1.md`'s best structural sentence - "the player is doing her work and
  calling it a rescue" - survives meeting 2 without a word changed.
- **The antagonist becomes genuinely nameless in World 1**, which is what
  `WORLD-1.md` wanted anyway. It is a sign, not a noun. The mark on the walls
  belongs to the thing that struck the name out.
- **The vessel motif gets its third term for free.** A queen in her mother's
  sarcophagus, an archaeologist with something else inside her, and a man walking
  around in a body that a spirit stood up.
- **Nothing already written has to be thrown away.** The four glyphs, the Serdab

# WHAT WORLD 1 NEEDS FROM THE MAP LANE

The owner's instruction: more space, and the descent should shape the map.

**The full brief - every dramatic purpose World 1 needs, which existing room
carries it, what goes deeper and in what order, where the fixed beats land, and
four warnings - is in `docs/WORLD-1.md` under "THE MAP BRIEF".** It is written in
purposes rather than geometry and it specifies no coordinates, because the map
lane knows constraints this lane does not.

Three findings from it belong in the story document, because they are conclusions
about the STORY rather than about the map.

**1. The story does not need more interior rooms.** Every dramatic purpose in
World 1 already has a home among the nine that exist. Adding rooms to make the
map feel bigger would produce exactly the thing the owner is complaining about: a
space that exists because the map wanted another room.

**2. What the story does need is outdoors.** Meeting 2 put a government, a team
of seven, equipment and a classified file into World 1's back story, and there is
currently nowhere outside for any of it to be. **The expedition camp is the one
genuinely new space World 1 requires**, and it is the only place in the entire
trilogy where the institution that caused all of this is physically present
before Area 51.

**3. World 1's floor is the Serdab, and what is under it is stone Egyptians did
not cut, and under that is the gate his team opened.** That is the answer to
"where does World 1 end and what is beneath it", and it is the only geographic
fact the map lane needs from the story.

> **RECOMMENDATION on the vertical question, mine: ONE real descent in World 1,
> not nine.** Author the per-room depth number as data and build exactly one
> place where the player physically goes down and can feel it, at the Act 2 to
> Act 3 break. If every room is a floor lower the player stops registering any of
> them, and the world already spends its one vertical gasp on a shaft that points
> UP and delivers nothing. One built descent against one built ascent is a shape.
> Nine floors is a number.
